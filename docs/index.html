mailcleaner indexes the headers of your mail on your own computer, shows you which senders are filling it up, and lets you archive, trash or unsubscribe in bulk. Every action shows a preview first. Nothing is ever permanently deleted.

How it works

Type mclean.
Your browser does the rest.

mailcleaner opens a page served from your own computer. Everything happens there, and you never need another command.

  1. 01

    Add a mailbox

    Pick your provider. The page shows its setup steps and links to where you create an app password, or for Outlook, gives you a code to sign in with. Add as many as you like.

  2. 02

    Look around

    It syncs headers right away. Overview, Attention, Senders, Cleanup, Domains, Categories and Age show where your mail comes from.

  3. 03

    Turn on actions

    When you're ready, click the Read-only pill. Archive, trash, label, mark read and unsubscribe unlock, and every bulk action still shows a preview first.

  4. 04

    Automate

    Save searches as rules, unsubscribe from the loud senders, and if you want, let the optional Assistant suggest what to protect or ignore.

Safety

It works on your real mailbox,
so it's careful about it.

These aren't settings. They're rules the code is built around, and the project's contributing guide refuses changes that break them.

Sync fetches headers, flags, labels and size into a SQLite file in ~/.mailcleaner, one per mailbox. A body is fetched only when you ask to preview one message, and it's never saved. Your mail server stays the source of truth: delete the index and the next sync rebuilds it.

  • No permanent delete, anywhere

    "Trash" means your provider's own Trash, where you can still get it back. The app never sends EXPUNGE and never sets \Deleted, and CI rejects code that tries. If a server can't MOVE, archive and trash are refused rather than faked with copy-then-delete.

  • Read-only until you say so

    Each mailbox starts read-only. Turning actions on asks you to confirm. Turning them off doesn't.

  • Protected mail stays out of bulk actions

    Finance, security, travel, orders, people you write to, and anything starred. Unread mail with a deadline in it stays protected until you've read it.

  • A preview before every bulk action

    How many messages, the date span, what categories they are, how many protected ones were left out, and examples. Then you decide.

  • Everything is logged and undoable

    Every batch lands in History. Undo is one click there, or straight from the toast after you act.

  • Mailboxes never mix

    Each one has its own index, rules, history and credential. No query in the app spans two mailboxes.

  • Nothing phones home

    No telemetry, analytics or crash reports. The page is bound to 127.0.0.1 and needs a one-time token on every request. The only outside connections are your mail server, an unsubscribe you confirmed, and an AI provider you chose to set up.

What's in it

Where your mail comes from,
and what to do about it.

The overview tells you how big the problem is. The rest of the app helps you shrink it, a batch at a time.

01 / PROVIDERS

Gmail, and nearly everything else.

Add as many mailboxes as you like, from different providers, and switch between them from the menu next to the logo.

For Gmail there's no Google Cloud project, OAuth client or consent screen. You make an app password, and if you revoke it, mailcleaner loses all access. Passwords go to your system keychain.

On Gmail, actions change labels and messages never move. Everywhere else a message lives in one folder, so labelling files it into the folder you name, and undo moves it back.

02 / CLASSIFIER

Sorted offline, without an AI model.

Every message gets a category, how much attention it wants now, and what should happen to it. The rules are plain heuristics: list headers, the sender's address, known domains, subject keywords, your provider's own labels, starred and age.

It also reads your Sent mail. Anyone you've written to counts as a correspondent and is protected, unless they're a mailing list.

Subject keywords come in packs for English, India, US, UK, German, French and Spanish mail. Settings has an accuracy check: correct thirty random messages and it scores itself, with missed protection reported first.

03 / RULES

Rules that suggest before they act.

A rule is a filter plus an action. Build one from Rules → New rule, or save any plain-English search as one.

New rules only suggest. Open one and you get the usual preview. Mark it auto and it runs when you click Apply auto rules, or on a background schedule you set up.

Rules can archive, mark read, label or star. They can't trash, and they skip protected mail.

04 / UNSUBSCRIBE

Unsubscribe from the loud ones.

The Unsubscribe page lists senders that offer it, loudest first. Where a sender supports one-click unsubscribe, a single request goes from your machine and that's it. Otherwise their page, or a pre-filled email, opens for you to finish.

Unsubscribe & archive also clears their backlog, as an ordinary batch you can undo.

Don't unsubscribe from spam. It tells the sender your address is real.

Because the link comes from the sender
  1. https only. Anything else is refused.
  2. Public address, pinned. The host has to resolve to a public IP, and the connection sticks to that address.
  3. No cookies, no redirects. One POST, nothing followed.
  4. Only the host is logged. The full link usually has a token that identifies you.
05 / ASSISTANT

An AI that can only make suggestions.

Off by default. Turn it on and it looks at senders the classifier isn't sure about, or ones that are loud and unprotected, and suggests a rule for each: protect, ignore, or a better category.

Nothing applies until you accept it. It can add protection but never remove it, and it's told nothing about senders it wasn't asked about. These limits are checked in code, not just asked for in the prompt.

With the Assistant on, the Ask box also takes plain English, like "unread newsletters older than a year". It becomes a filter from a fixed field list, checked by the server. Never SQL.

FAQ

Questions people ask

Is the source code available?

Yes. It's on GitHub under the MIT license, so you can read exactly what it does with your mailbox, or build it yourself. If you'd like to support the project, there's a Buy Me a Coffee page.

Can it delete my email?

No. There's no permanent delete in the code for any provider. Trash moves mail to your provider's own Trash folder, where it stays recoverable, and you can undo the batch from History. If your server can't move messages, mailcleaner refuses to archive or trash instead of copying and deleting.

Does my mail leave my computer?

Headers come from your mail server into a local SQLite file, and stay there. There's no telemetry. The exceptions are ones you start: a one-click unsubscribe sends one request to the sender, and if you set up the Assistant with Claude, sender statistics and up to five subject lines per sender go to Anthropic. With Ollama, nothing leaves your machine.

Why an app password instead of "Sign in with Google"?

It keeps Google Cloud out of it entirely: no OAuth client, no consent screen, no app review. The app password lives in your system keychain and is only sent to Gmail's IMAP server. Revoke it on your Google account page and mailcleaner loses all access.

What about Outlook and Microsoft 365?

Microsoft turned off password sign-in for IMAP, so mailcleaner shows a short code to enter at microsoft.com/devicelogin. The only permission it asks for is IMAP access to your own mailbox, and you can revoke it from your Microsoft account. If your organisation blocks third-party apps, you can register your own and point mailcleaner at it.

Does it work with Proton Mail?

Yes, through Proton Mail Bridge, which needs a paid Proton plan. Bridge decrypts mail locally and gives you an IMAP host, port and password to use.

Can I use it from the terminal instead?

Yes. Everything in the browser app also works as commands, for scripts or if you prefer it, and mclean ui opens a terminal dashboard. The terminal is never required. The old gclean command still works as an alias.

How do I uninstall it?

On macOS and Linux, delete ~/.local/bin/mclean, then ~/.mailcleaner for the local indexes. On Windows the program lives in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\mailcleaner. Removing the index doesn't touch your mail.
